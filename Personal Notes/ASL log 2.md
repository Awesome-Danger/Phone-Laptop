1987 National ASL Poetry Conference Performance: Clayton Valli 1:27:29

watched: 10/24/26 10:55am

(this is a much longer video, so I think it’s only right the response be as well).

I enjoyed the poems. Clayton’s works have a lot of waviness to them, and he tends to use nature to reflect emotions, rather than preferring a rural setting. Even in _Cave_, the inside starts out as a cave, and turns into a nice place for a banquet, but it still starts off with nature. The quality of the video is a bit unfortunate, as well as the visibility during the presentation, but this was the late 80s, so resolution with commercial colored video still needed some work; I also think the camera worker wasn’t paying attention to the camera for some parts. During the presentation, I found some of his points interesting about how one might create line structure, and rhyming in ASL poetry. I’ve thought to myself already as to how one might structure a poem in ASL, and figured the use of similar handshapes and movements would be considered rhyming, while the flow, beat/rhythm and/or structures of _how_ the poem is signed/produced would be something akin to line structure and stanzaic form.

Deaf Poets Society | Douglas Ridloff | TEDxVienna 13:01

watched: 10/24/26 2:55pm

I have seen this poet before, and I love the idea that you can use poetry without words, and still convey a meaning that can be interpreted in a myriad of ways. I’ve seen the first poem before, and I think it’s about the journey of going from deaf to Deaf; a hard journey of learning language later in life than you should have, followed by a feeling of freedom like flying. The second one is a lot harder to pin down for me. It might be a commentary on the limitations of speaking, and the fluidity that ASL offers in comparison, being able to morph from one thing to another.

Here I Am to Worship in ASL & CC by Rock Church Deaf Ministry 3:49

watched: 10/24/26 4:28

Some of the sign choices that stuck out to me, like “let me see” as an open 8 HS to convey an emotional sight, rather than eyesight; the other ones are for the phrases “all for love’s sake,” as “I love you” sweeping outward; “how much it cost,” as “suffering;” and “say that (...you’re my God)” as a specific type of “proclaim.” The consistency and beat are very evident in the production of the signing as well.
