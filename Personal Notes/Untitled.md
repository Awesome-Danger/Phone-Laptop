102 - I think that there's a place and time to hide your Christian faith, and that's when you're just starting to build in a place that persecutes Christians, and will snuff you out. Maybe I got the wrong impression but it seems that people nearby the catacomb believers are publicly Christian, so why should they hide? 
I don't think it's wrong to keep certain cultural holidays and customs, as long as they are true to the Bible, and the Christian faith; but if family relations are keeping you back, and from being true to God, then it's not worth it.

103 - the fact that you don't have to go through Jewish culture to get to heaven means there is a huge amount of leeway when it comes to culture that is acceptable. Early Jewish culture involved singing, instruments, dancing, certain ways of mourning, and other petitions for prayer, like fasting. We, as Westerners, don't follow that culture, even though a lot of it was acceptable, and even Jesus did it. Western Christian culture, or Church of Christ culture is not the only way into heaven.
I don't like how she is distinguishing religious conversion, and "going through Christ without having to put on the Christian label." "Christian" is just what the 1st century church called themselves.

9 - I disagree that "learning God's ways may be more important than making sincere attempts to do His will." Sincerity matters a ton! Rev. 3:15-16, love, forgiveness, prayer, the Holy Spirit, the whole thing about the intent of the law and not just the letter of it, it's all about sincerity! It's all meaningless and empty if you have no sincerity. "...let love be sincere..." To learn to follow God's ways, you need to do God's will in some way, like reading the Bible, or going to church. God's ways and will overlap greatly, so trying to say that one is greater than the other feels wrong. The writer might be saying that because you learn God's will through His ways, that that's more important, but I feel that's in inappropriate way of putting it.

43 - I feel that single missionaries should be more careful as to how they tell people about the roles of men and women in the church. Not that I think that these women being shown a new outlook on life is a bad thing--far from it. But I worry that the women teaching them don't have the experience and wisdom to tell and show these women what a good, healthy, Christ centered marriage or relationship looks like, or what roles women have that are unique and/or common from men in both the family, and as children of God. I worry that their preconceived worldviews will poison the relationships between these women and the men in their lives like it is here in the U.S.. They might have the gift of utterance of knowledge and wisdom talked about in 1Cor 12:8, but something tells me no.
