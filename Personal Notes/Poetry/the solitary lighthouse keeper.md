poem about solitary lighthouse keeper

night cold and dark, the air creeps up arms 
sticky and humid it swarms on his skin
head swimming with sleep, it could do no harm.
rain rap, tap, tapping, creating a din,
the biting cold fighting to get in.

there's no souls to be heard, begging, crying
for a red-white savior to light their way.
now and forever, a bright eye spying
searching for one in the watery frey;
perhaps they will cry for help someday

cold mist, urgently waiting in the glass,
warm tongues from the furnace becoming slack.
there's no harm allowing the night to pass,
the keeper holds his eyes open a crack,
the chill of death can't enter this shack.

the man in his chair, eyes heavy with sleep,
the lighthouse stands strong, amidst waters fast
guarding the souls out in the waters deep;
but one drowning and cold atop the mast
a keeper no more, the man breathed his last
