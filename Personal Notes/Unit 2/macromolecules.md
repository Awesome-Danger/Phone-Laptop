## Carbon
- Carbon is the backbone of all life
- Organic molecules contain carbon
- Has 4 valence electrons and can bond easily with itself or other atoms
- Methane is the simplest carbon compound with one carbon and 4 hydrogen atoms attached

*basically*
**organized by first source of energy to last**
1. Carbohydrates
2. Lipids

4. Nucleic Acids
## Carbohydrates
- Made up of carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen in a 1:2:1 ratio
- Building block is the monosaccharide
- Example is glucose which has the formula of C6H12O6
- Many of our carbohydrates are long chains of glucose molecules
- 2 monosaccharides together is a disaccharide such as sucrose
	- most of the carbohydrates in your body don't exist as a monosaccharide
- Many monosaccharides together is known as a polysaccharides (typically more than 100) such as starch, glycogen, and cellulose
- Carbohydrates typically end with “ose” at the end

- *Isomers* of glucose such as fructose and lactose
- An *isomer* is a molecule with the same chemical formula but different structural arrangements
- Bonding occurs between monosaccharides when a dehydration synthesis reaction occurs and a molecule of water is formed *(an H atom from one monosaccharide binds to an OH- group from the other molecule)*
	- **dehydration synthesis $\to$ how polysaccharide is made**

- Carbohydrates are the first energy source for living things
- Plants store this energy as *starch* and animals store this energy as *glycogen*
	- plants $\to$ starch  /  animals $\to$ glycogen
- Cellulose is a structural carbohydrate found in the cell walls of plant cells; it is believed to be the most abundant carbohydrate on earth
	- **humans' digestive system can't digest cellulose**, but some of the bacteria in your gut can partially break down some of it.
- Humans store glycogen in our liver and in our muscles

## Lipids (fats)
Made of carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen; with a different ratio of Oxygen atoms than the others
- Insoluble in water (hydrophobic)
	- Functions:
	- Energy source
	- Insulator
	- Hormones
	- Water repellent (water fowl)
	- steroids
		- Contains:
			- Fats
			- Oils and waxes
			- Steroids
			- ring structure
			- phospholipids

![[cholesteral.jpg]]

### Fats
- Made up of triglycerides
- Triglycerides=**1 glycerol molecule with 3 fatty acid chains**
- Fatty acid chains average 16 to 18 carbons in each chain, but can have anywhere from 4 to 32 in a chain
- Chains can be either saturated or unsaturated
- Stored in adipocytes in animals and usually seeds in plants

### Saturated vs. Unsaturated
- **Saturated fats $\to$ maximum number of Hydrogen atoms attached to the carbons**
- These typically form straight chains which makes them easier to pack together
- Therefore, they are **solids at room temperature**; *typical animal fat* (Lard)
- *Unsaturated fats* are missing a hydrogen, causing to have some double bonds in them causing them to kink/bend and are difficult to pack together
- Therefore, they are oils *(liquid) at room temperature*
- Typical plant fats

### Phospholipids
- Made up of a glycerol, 2 fatty acid chains and a phosphate molecule.
- Similar to triglyceride except a phosphate group takes the place of one of the fatty acid chains
- Main component of our cell membranes
- Has a hydrophilic head (water loving) and a hydrophobic (water fearing) tail
- Form membrane by orienting the fatty acids to the middle and the phosphate head to the outside where the water would be
- Double layer in our membranes

### Steroids
- 4 ring structure
- Insoluble in water (reason why they are considered a lipid)
- Cholesterol is main example
	- Important part of our cell membranes
	- Helps maintain the structure of the membrane when conditions are difficult
- Can be precursor to hormones and vitamins (E and K)

### Waxes
- Similar to oils but thicker
- Examples:
	- Honey from bees
	- Cuticles on plants

## Proteins
- Made up of chains of Amino Acids
- The number and sequence of Amino acids determine the protein’s function
- Therefore, **Amino Acids are the building blocks of proteins**
- Functions of proteins:
	- Structure (top function) (collagen)
	- Enzymes (all are proteins)
	- Hormones (can be proteins)
	- Transport (can hook up to cell parts, and hook onto them)
	- defense (skin is huge for defense; separated and held together by proteins)
	- contraction (muscles, red blood cells $\to$ change shape to fit through small holes (capillaries))

### Amino Acids
- There approximately 20 Amino acids (some say 21)
- Approximately 8 of these are essential
- All amino acids have **4 main components** surrounding a carbon molecule:
	- all are exactly the same, except for 1 part
- An amine group NH2
- A carboxyl group-COOH
- An H atom
- An **R group or variable group** (this is the group that separates the different types of amino acids; it's what makes them different)
- Amino acids **form a peptide bond by dehydration synthesisforms** (reaction) when an OH from the carboxyl and an H from the amine group form a molecule of water

![[amino-acid.jpg]]

### Protein confirmations (shapes)
- Confirmations of proteins
- Primary
	- **linear chain** of amino acids-polypeptide chain
- Secondary
	- **chain folds into a helix or pleated sheet**
- Tertiary
	- When the **coil or pleated sheet folds in on itself**
- Quartenary
	- When **two or more folded chains join together** to form a *globular protein*
	- Proper shape and folding is important to the function of a protein
	- Improperly folded proteins will be destroyed

## Nucleic Acids
- Carry the blueprint to pass on the characteristics of life
- Can be [[DNA]] or [[RNA]]
- Differences:
	- DNA has Deoxyribose sugar; RNA has ribose sugar
	- DNA is a double strand; RNA is a single strand
	- DNA contains the bases A,T,C,G; RNA contains the bases A,C,U,G


- Building block is a nucleotide
- Nucleotide has 3 parts
- Sugar-either ribose or deoxyribose
	- (shape of an L)
- Phosphate group
- Nitrogen base
- Nitrogen bases:
- Adenine (A always matches with T)
- Thymine
- Cytosine (C always matches with G)
- Guanine
1. A nucleotide is made up of three components: a nitrogenous base, a pentose sugar, and a phosphate group.

### DNA
- DNA is a double stranded nucleic acid
- It is a chain of nucleotides joined together into two complimentary strands
- Notice that A always matches with T and C always matches with G
