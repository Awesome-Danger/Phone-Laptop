- Smallest unit of an element that still has the properties of that element
Atoms are made up of three things:
- Protons
- Neutrons
- Electrons
	- Protons and neutrons are found in the *nucleus* of the atom
	- Electrons are found in orbits (energy levels) around the nucleus
	- Protons (+); electrons (-) and neutrons ()
	- *Stable atoms have equal numbers of protons and electrons*

- The sequential number on the periodic table tells the number of protons in an atom
- Since protons and electrons are equal in a stable atom the atomic number tells us that information as well
- The atomic mass is the other number on the periodic table
- This mass is the protons and neutrons added together
- We can determine the number of neutrons by subtracting the atomic number from the atomic mass

- Electrons are arranged in energy shells (levels)
- The first shell has a maximum of 2, the second has a maximum of 8, the third 18 with some exceptions
- **outer electron shell = valence shell**
- valence electrons will help to determine the reactivity of the particular atom
- Columns = number of electrons in the outer shell
	- Column 1 (of the periodic table) would have 1 in outer shell and column 18 has 8 in its outer shell
- We *ignore the transition elements (columns 3-12)* and concentrate on columns 1,2, and 13-18

### reactivity
- Reactivity is determined by the valence electrons
	- Atoms tend to lose or gain electrons
	- If they lose an electron they become a cation or positive ion
	- If they gain an electron then the become an anion or negative ion
	- Example:
		- Na, sodium tends to lose its only valence electron and becom a cation
		- Chlorine tends to add an electron to its 7 valence electrons and become an anion)then is usually referred to as chloride

### reactions and equations
- Chemical reactions are represented by chemical equations.
Types of reactions:
- **Synthesis**: A+B=AB
- **Decomposition**:AB=A+B
- **Substitution**: AB +C=AC+B

### bonding
- Three types of bonding:
	- Ionic
	- Covalent
		- both polar and non-polar
	- Hydrogen
#### ionic bonding
- Occurs when two oppositely charged ions are attracted to each other and join
- Example:
	- +1 (Na) joins with -1 (Cl) to form $NaCl$ or sodium chloride
	- Therefore atoms in column 1 and in column 17 would be very reactive with each other
	- +2 (Mg) is in the 2nd column, so it has 2 additional electrons, making just bonding with one +1 (Cl) negatively charged, so it ends up becoming $MgCl_2$ 

#### covalent bonding
- Occurs when atoms share electrons
- Easier to share then to lose multiple electrons
- **Strong bonds**
- Can share 1,2 or 3 pairs of electrons
- Known as single, double and triple bonds
- Can be shared equally or non equally
	- Equal=non polar-usually linear
	- Unequal=polar bonds-slight charges on each end of molecule such as a water molecule-hydrogen is slightly positive and the oxygen is slightly negative

#### hydrogen bonding
- Weaker bonds
- Gives water many of its important properties
- Slight positive and negative charges attract
	- technically water is a hydrogen bond

#### Van der Waals forces
- Weakest attraction
- Attraction when electrons in outer shell pass by and no electron is present in nearby shell
- Proximity to another molecule is important
